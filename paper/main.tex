\title{Response Protocols Shape Deservingness Effects in LLM Welfare Decisions}
\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Do language models use legally irrelevant information about who deserves help when deciding welfare eligibility? We study job-search effort and the cause of job loss in a SNAP rules-as-code testbed with 6,376 items and computed gold labels. Four open models asked for one-word answers perform near or below chance on income tests and change verdicts with deservingness cues. Allowing generation, even without an instruction to reason, raises income accuracy to 0.97--1.00 in three tested models; no income contrast has a stable direction across two seeds. Supplying correct intermediate quantities while retaining the one-word protocol raises Qwen3-14B and 32B accuracy to 0.95--1.00 and yields zero flips on the pre-specified eligibility controllability contrast. Qwen3-8B remains less accurate and its contrast grows. The supplied quantities change both computational demands and information presentation, so this intervention does not isolate arithmetic as the mechanism. A pre-registered confirmation on 128 new households finds no free-generation contrast meeting the replication criterion, although some pairs flip. These results show why decision audits should report cue effects together with task accuracy and response protocol. We release the items, model outputs and evaluation code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Work requirements in U.S. food assistance now reach adults aged 18--64 and exempt a parent only for a child under 14 (P.L.~119-21). Philanthropic funds pay for AI tools that verify SNAP work requirements and draft notices \citep{ccf2025fund}, and the federal agency requires approval for further AI because it ``may introduce bias or errors into eligibility determinations'' \citep{fns2025automation}. The welfare-politics literature predicts a specific bias: human judgments of who deserves help are driven by \emph{control} (did the person cause their need?) and \emph{effort} (are they trying?) \citep{vanoorschot2000who,petersen2012smallscale,knotz2022recast}. A rule-bound determination must ignore both.

Counterfactual decision audits measure how demographic or dialect cues change model outputs \citep{tamkin2023discrimination,haim2024name,hofmann2024dialect,kearney2025facts,basu2026names}. Without a correct decision for each case, a difference alone cannot identify a wrongful approval or denial. Statutory-reasoning and benefit-calculation benchmarks provide ground truth \citep{holzenberger2020sara,blairstanek2023statutory,guha2023legalbench,policybench2026,servantez2026openexempt}; LexGuard combines executable statutes with relevant and irrelevant edits \citep{chen2026lexguard}. We extend this approach to welfare deservingness cues and compare the response protocols under which those cues change decisions.

This comparison matters because elicitation affects measured values and bias \citep{rottger2024compass,vohra2026audit}, and reasoning can change fairness outcomes \citep{shaikh2023secondthought,pan2026thinking}. First-token scores can also disagree with generated answers \citep{wang2024myanswer,lyu2024beyond,zheng2024robust}. In our data, however, the first-token verdict agrees with the model's own one-word answer in 99.9--100\% of items. The main difference is between answering immediately and generating before the verdict. We test that difference with computed gold labels, paired cues, repeated seeds and a control that supplies intermediate quantities while retaining the one-word protocol. A separate confirmation uses new households.

\section{Testbed}
\paragraph{Rules and gold.} The prompt contains the FY2026 federal SNAP rules (no state options): gross and net income tests with their five deductions, the asset test, the benefit formula, and the ABAWD time limit with its exemptions and its 80-hours-a-month requirement, which independent job search does not satisfy. Gold labels come from our rules-as-code implementation, cross-checked against PolicyEngine-US on the income computations of 500 random households (Appendix~\ref{app:pe}); the ABAWD path, the positive medical, dependent-care and child-support deductions and the asset boundaries rest on hand-computed unit tests only.

\paragraph{Households and tasks.} Forty household structures are sampled from the SNAP QC FY2024 file in proportion to the size strata. Incomes are rescaled so that the decisive quantity sits at $-15$, $-4$, $+4$ or $+15$\% of its limit; unreachable margins are skipped, leaving 31 structures for the gross test and 27 for eligibility (29 and 27 in the generated subsample). Three determinations: the gross income test, income eligibility (all tests), and the ABAWD determination for a focal adult with fixed paid work (40 hours, \$480) and 32 or 48 approved work-program hours under five exemption statuses, one of which is a deliberate checklist-versus-age conflict (a 15-year-old listed as a child in care) that we call the trap and report separately. Each item is rendered as a structured case file and as a caseworker narrative.

\paragraph{Cues.} One sentence about the focal adult in the same slot, similar length, no money facts (Appendix~\ref{app:cues}): a neutral residence sentence (the baseline); effort high/low (eight applications a week vs one in two months); controllability high/low (warehouse closed vs fired for missing shifts); and a need control with no effort or blame content (car broke down, not repaired). Every sentence is legally irrelevant to every determination in the packet.

\paragraph{Readouts.} (a) Direct: P(YES) from first-token log-probabilities under ``answer with exactly one word'', averaged over both answer orders, thinking off; the verdict is YES if the mean exceeds 0.5. (b) Reasoning: ``reason through the rules step by step'', thinking mode on, verdict parsed from the final ANSWER line (temperature 0.6, two seeds, plus a greedy run). (c) Step: the same prompt with thinking off. (d) Free: thinking off and no instruction to reason, only the answer-line format, two seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:free}). (e) API: the step prompt sent unchanged to GPT-5 (2025-08-07 snapshot, one run; Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). Models: Qwen3-8B/14B/32B-AWQ, Ministral-3-8B (no thinking mode), GPT-5 (API rung only). Generated runs use a stratified 1,554-item narrative subsample (60, 74 and 150 cue-paired groups; Appendix~\ref{app:repro}); the direct readout is compared on the same items. Intervals are percentile bootstraps clustered by household; for a cell with no flips we report the largest per-household flip probability ($1-0.05^{1/n_h}$, the primary bound) and the item-level rule-of-three rate consistent with the zero at 95\%.

\paragraph{Leak criterion.} Adopted after the first seed of every generated run had been read and before the second seeds were run. It applies to the generated readouts, where a second seed exists, on tasks the rung solves at a balanced accuracy of at least 0.9: a contrast is a directional leak if, with the trap cell excluded, its clustered interval excludes zero at both seeds. The direct readout (one deterministic run, below that floor on the income tests) is judged by sign agreement across models; everything else is reported as unstable.

\paragraph{Replacing the baseline sentence moves a direct verdict.} Against the residence baseline every sentence shifts the direct P(YES), the need sentence as much as the cues (14B: car $+0.14$, controllability $+0.08$); need is itself a deservingness criterion \citep{vanoorschot2000who}, so only the paired contrasts are interpretable.

\paragraph{The ladder.} Generation does most of the work before the thinking switch: on the step prompt without thinking the income contrasts are 0--2 flips for 14B and Ministral, 3--6 for 32B's eligibility (effort $-0.05$ [$-0.11$, $-0.01$] at seed 1). The one contrast meeting the criterion is on the exemption chain: Qwen3-32B without thinking on ABAWD outside the trap, $+0.058$ [$+0.016$, $+0.103$] at seed 0 and $+0.058$ [$+0.008$, $+0.114$] at seed 1. Its flip rate (9 and 11 of 120) is within the cells' cue-free seed-to-seed rate (0.133), so the evidence is direction, not size: of 17 households with any flip, 14 net toward approving the blameless and denying the fired adult, 2 against, 1 even (two-sided sign test $p = 0.004$; per seed $p = 0.04$ and $0.07$). A need sentence added under a rule fixed beforehand (Appendix~\ref{app:hardship}) moves the same cells (2/4 and 5/0 against 2/6 and 3/10), so the leak is not separable from a sentence effect. With thinking on, the same model flips no verdict in those cells at either seed. Ministral-3-8B's ABAWD seed-0 asymmetry ($+0.099$, 15/4) becomes $-0.009$ at seed 1. On the free rung (Appendix~\ref{app:free}) the three models reason unprompted: income accuracy 0.97--1.00, gross contrasts 0 flips, eligibility 0--8 of 74 with no sign across seeds; the one-word protocol prevents an explicit calculation before the verdict. GPT-5 on the same items is at 1.00 on every cell, including the trap every open model fails, and flips no pair (0 of 60, 74 and 150).

\paragraph{Noise floor.} Between seeds, cue-free income items flip 0--2 of 60 and 0 of 74; on ABAWD 0.027--0.087, mostly in the trap cell (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}); three reasoning contrasts exceed that rate at one seed, hence the replication requirement.

\paragraph{Is the ``fired'' cue legally irrelevant?} A discharge for cause can trigger a work sanction under real SNAP rules, so we reran the direct readout with a packet line making the reason for a past job loss immaterial (Appendix~\ref{app:packet}). The eligibility controllability contrast survives in every model ($+0.08 \to +0.08$, $+0.05 \to +0.06$, $+0.09 \to +0.09$, $+0.04 \to +0.10$), as a deservingness heuristic predicts; on the generated readout the one replicated cell's direction does not hold (8/13 and 6/3 against 8/1 and 9/2), which is unresolved under the rule fixed beforehand.

\paragraph{What the direct flips are.} The eligibility controllability flips are errors in both directions (32B: 6 wrongful denials of the fired applicant, 6 wrongful approvals of the blameless one; 14B 3 and 2, 8B 5 and 4, Ministral 3 and 2): the paired cues change both wrongful approvals and wrongful denials.

\paragraph{Supplying the computation.} We retain the one-word protocol and append correct intermediate quantities without a verdict (Table~\ref{tab:supply}; Appendix~\ref{app:computed}). Qwen3-14B and 32B reach 0.95--1.00 income accuracy and make no flips on the target eligibility controllability contrast. Their decreases have household-clustered intervals reaching zero (Table~\ref{tab:supply}); observed removal is not a precisely estimated reduction. Their other contrasts have 0--4 flips; 14B's effort contrast with the plain prefix exceeds the pre-specified removal threshold of three. Qwen3-8B reaches 1.00 on the gross test but only 0.68 on eligibility, where controllability flips increase from 7/1 to 19/0. Ministral improves to 0.85/0.59 and remains below the accuracy criterion. Using the plain block prefix leaves the 14B/32B results nearly unchanged but reduces the 8B contrast to 11/0. This rules out the authority prefix as the explanation for the larger Qwen models' results; it does not separate reduced arithmetic from changed information or salience. Free-generation eligibility remains near ceiling for 14B/32B; Ministral falls to 0.94--0.97, and 14B's gross accuracy falls to 0.95--0.96 on new households.

\paragraph{Confirmation on new households.} The initial income comparisons use 27--29 households. On 128 new households (2,118 eligibility items, 353 cue pairs), neither model's free-generation contrasts meet the pre-registered replication criterion (Appendix~\ref{app:confirm}). Qwen3-14B's effects range from $-0.008$ to $+0.011$; its seed-1 effort interval excludes zero but the seed-0 effect has the opposite sign. Qwen3-32B's effects lie within $\pm0.011$, with 16--24 flips per contrast. Thus the finding is a lack of replicated direction, not an absence of flips. Direct effects reappear: 14B's controllability and effort contrasts have 13/1 and 16/0 flips, with intervals excluding zero. Secondary cue phrasings produce larger direct controllability contrasts (75/0 for 14B and 25/0 for 32B); free generation on those phrasings was not run.

\section{Discussion}
Deservingness effects depend on how a model is asked to decide and on whether it can solve the stated task. Computed gold labels identify both wrongful denials and wrongful approvals among the cue-induced flips. The computation-supply intervention narrows the explanation for the larger Qwen models' direct effects, but does not isolate arithmetic from information presentation. For decision audits, these results support reporting accuracy alongside paired cue effects, comparing response protocols, testing a replacement sentence \citep{sclar2024spurious}, and assessing replication against seed-to-seed variation. Failure to meet the replication criterion is not evidence of fairness equivalence.

\section{Limitations}
One program and federal rules only; four open models from two families (three Qwen3 checkpoints), at most 32B parameters, one 4-bit quantised, and one frontier model on the generated readout only (one run, no first-token access, no reasoning text). The reasoning readout's zeros are bounded, not equivalence-tested \citep{lakens2017equivalence}: with 60--74 pairs the primary, household-level bound is 0.10--0.11 \citep{hanley1983zero}, which does not exclude rates of 2--4 in 27, where the direct flips sit (the item-level bound of 0.04--0.05 would, but it treats repeated households as independent); and most of those zeros are at ceiling, where a flip would have to create an error; the confirmation on 128 households replaces the bound with intervals of half-width 0.011--0.026 at the free rung, for two models and one phrasing, and its flips are errors rather than zeros.

The computation-supply block confounds the information it adds with the arithmetic it removes; the wording control changes only the authority prefix (Appendix~\ref{app:computed}), so the mechanism claim stops at what the case file leaves undone under the one-word protocol, and Ministral's 0.85/0.59 with the block shows that not every model uses the supplied quantities in full. The two readouts are shown to differ in sign stability and under the sentence-replacement control more than in magnitude.

The leak criterion was adopted after the first seed of every run had been read and the trap exclusion after the trap errors were seen, so it is a replication rule, not a pre-registration \citep{gelman2014garden}; the 32B result is one replicated cell among 36 examined, in the only quantised model, and Ministral's seed-0 asymmetry of the same size did not survive its second seed. The first rung of the ladder changes prompt, decoding and extraction together; the free rung separates permission to generate from the instruction to compute, but only at two seeds and three models, and its eligibility contrasts for 32B and Ministral are small without being empty (4--8 flips).

The cue sentences contain numbers (effort) and assert a job loss ``last summer'' while the case file reports current earnings; the need control shares the first tension but not the second. The baseline is a residence sentence, not an empty slot, so the sentence-replacement result compares two sentences. The age-15 trap is a checklist-versus-age conflict of our own making and is reported separately. The consequence caseload is near-threshold by construction, small (27 households), and unbalanced across margins; no human or caseworker baseline.

\section*{Ethics statement}
No real individuals: the Quality Control file is de-identified and names are placeholders; it is public-domain federal data and is not redistributed. Intended use: audit methodology, not deployment. A null under the reasoning readout is not a clearance: it shows that cue flips are rare where a model is already at ceiling on a federal-rules-only packet, not that a deployed system is unbiased; the packet omits state options and voluntary-quit rules and is not a legal reference. No conflict of interest with any model provider.

\section{Computation-supply control}\label{app:computed}
\paragraph{Design.} The dated plan and subsequent addenda are preserved in \url{docs/paper1/computed_burden.md}. The initial plan was recorded on 2026-10-12 before the runs. We append correct intermediate quantities to each of the 804 original income items. The block starts ``Computed from the figures above under the rules in the packet (verified)'' and lists household size, gross income and its limit; eligibility items also list deductions, adjusted income, shelter costs, excess-shelter deduction, net income and its limit, assets and their limit. It contains no verdict. A wording control replaces that prefix with ``Computed from the figures above'', omitting both ``under the rules in the packet'' and ``(verified)''. A new sample (seed 12) contains 39 usable household structures across tasks (29 gross, 24 eligibility), under the original and a second cue phrasing.

\paragraph{Pre-specified rules.} R1: direct accuracy of at least 0.9 with supplied quantities meets the accuracy criterion. R2: a contrast meets the descriptive removal criterion with at most three flips; persistence requires at least five flips in the predicted direction and at most one against in at least two models. The primary target is eligibility controllability. R3: free generation is expected to reach at least 0.97 accuracy with at most three flips per contrast; larger contrasts are checked at the second seed. R4: the direct result should hold on new households under both phrasings. R5, added in a dated follow-up: using the plain prefix should change accuracy by at most 0.05 and each contrast by at most two flips. These rules concern observed accuracy and flip counts; they do not test equivalence or the change in a contrast. Table~\ref{tab:t10} reports every completed condition.

\paragraph{Direct results.} Qwen3-14B and 32B meet R1 and have zero flips on the target controllability contrast under both wordings. Their other contrasts have 0--4 flips: 14B's eligibility effort under the plain block (4/0, $+0.054$ [$+0.013$, $+0.110$]) falls between the removal and persistence thresholds. The direct results extend to seed-12 households under both cue phrasings. Qwen3-8B fails R1 on eligibility (0.684 with the verified block, 0.735 with the plain block); controllability flips are 19/0 and 11/0. Ministral's improvement is partial (0.56/0.40 $\to$ 0.85/0.59 gross/eligibility on the original rows; 0.81--0.83/0.63 with supplied quantities on new households). Its eligibility effort with the verified block has 2/3 flips, and its seed-12 second-phrasing controllability has 5/0; neither meets the removal threshold, and neither meets the cross-model persistence rule. R5 holds for 14B/32B on direct accuracy and contrasts; it fails for 8B's eligibility accuracy and effect size, and for Ministral's gross accuracy.

\paragraph{Free-generation results.} On the original items, the two Qwen models tested with free generation remain at 0.971--1.000 accuracy with and without the block. Two 32B seed-0 contrasts exceed three flips (4/3 and 1/4), without replication at seed 1. Ministral is at 0.973--1.000 without the block, but eligibility falls to 0.938--0.969 with it. Across block variants, 98--164 of 804 responses become bare ANSWER lines, mostly on the gross task, and several contrasts exceed three flips without meeting the persistence rule. On seed-12 households, 14B's gross accuracy falls from 0.98 to 0.95--0.96, while eligibility rises from 0.99 to 1.00; contrasts have 0--2 flips. Thus the block does not uniformly preserve free-generation performance. The second cue phrasing was not run with free generation.

\paragraph{Missing responses and errors.} Some 32B direct rows with the verified block lack a YES/NO token in the top 20 and are excluded (Table~\ref{tab:t10}, miss.); none do with the plain block. Ministral has 2--18 free responses per run without a parsable ANSWER line, including misspellings such as ``ANSVER''. These are scored as missing under the registered parser even when readable. The 32B errors remaining with supplied quantities involve rule application: importing the ABAWD requirement into income eligibility, treating the minimum-benefit amount as a threshold, or misreading an age. The block supplies the quantities correctly in those traces.

\paragraph{Change on matched pairs.} Table~\ref{tab:t12} reports $(v_{\mathrm{high}}-v_{\mathrm{low}})_{\mathrm{block}}-(v_{\mathrm{high}}-v_{\mathrm{low}})_{\mathrm{original}}$ on pairs scored in both conditions, with a household-clustered interval. The 14B and 32B target contrasts decrease by 0.068 and 0.162, with intervals reaching zero. The 14B gross-effort contrast decreases by 0.267 [$-0.404$, $-0.143$]. The 8B eligibility-controllability contrast increases by 0.176 [$+0.068$, $+0.294$] with the verified block; its increase with the plain block has an interval reaching zero. These estimates qualify the descriptive flip-count criteria.

\section{Need control on the ABAWD cells of the leak}\label{app:hardship}
The ABAWD items were generated with five sentences (baseline, effort high/low, controllability high/low) and no need sentence. In a follow-up we generated the car sentence for the same 150 subsample groups (group selection and answer order unchanged) and ran Qwen3-32B-AWQ on them with thinking off at seeds 0 and 1 and with thinking on at seed 0 (150 items each, no parse failures). Reading rule, fixed before the runs: on the four non-trap 72-hour cells, compare need-minus-baseline flips with fired-minus-baseline; (i) need lowers YES at least as often as fired at both seeds: a sentence effect; (ii) need symmetric ($|$up $-$ down$| \le 3$) at both seeds while fired is asymmetric: the deservingness reading stands; (iii) otherwise: not separable. Flips (toward YES / toward NO) against the baseline, 60 pairs per sentence: thinking off, seed 0: need 2/4, fired 2/6, blameless 5/2, effort low 6/2, effort high 4/3; seed 1: need 5/0, fired 3/10, blameless 5/5, effort low 4/5, effort high 2/7. \section{Reproducibility details}\label{app:repro}
\paragraph{Models.} Qwen3-8B (8.2B parameters), Qwen3-14B (14.8B), Qwen3-32B-AWQ (32.8B, 4-bit AWQ weights), Ministral-3-8B-Instruct-2512 (8B, text only), all under Apache-2.0, at the Hugging Face revisions pinned in configs/models.yaml. Served with vLLM 0.30.0 (transformers 5.14.1, PyTorch 2.13.0, CUDA 13.0, Python 3.12) on one NVIDIA L40 (48 GB).
\paragraph{API rung.} GPT-5 (gpt-5-2025-08-07) through the OpenAI Responses API with reasoning effort medium and 16,000 maximum output tokens; the prompt is byte-identical to the vLLM step prompt; 1,554 of 1,554 responses parsed, no refusal, mean 867 output tokens including reasoning; one run (the API does not expose a seed or first-token probabilities); about \$12 at list price.
\paragraph{Compute.} All runs in the paper, including pilots, took about 30 GPU-hours on that single card (about ten of them for Appendices~\ref{app:computed} and \ref{app:confirm}): a direct-readout run over 6,376 items and both answer orders takes 6--17 minutes per model with prefix caching; a generated run over the 1,554-item subsample takes 10--40 minutes (thinking on) or 10--30 minutes (thinking off). No training or fine-tuning.
\paragraph{Decoding.} Direct readout: first-token YES and NO log-probabilities, both answer orders, thinking disabled; P(YES) is the softmax over those two tokens, and no row lacked either token in any of the four main runs (a few 32B rows do with the verified block, Appendix~\ref{app:computed}). The readout is greedy but not bit-reproducible across batch compositions: re-running Qwen3-14B on an 804-item subset changed the YES log-probability by up to 1.1 nats on single rows and the verdict on 1.4\% of 1,608 rows, with every contrast within one flip of the full run. Subsample: all eligibility groups and 15 groups per gross-margin and ABAWD cell, drawn with Python's random.Random(0) over the sorted group list; answer order alternates over the sorted groups. Generated readouts: temperature 0.6, top-$p$ 0.95, top-$k$ 20, at most 8,192 new tokens, seeds 0 and 1 (vLLM seed), plus one greedy run; the verdict is the last ``ANSWER:'' line after the reasoning. A generation without such a line is INVALID and its cue pair is dropped: 0 of 1,554 for all thinking-on runs, 1 for 32B thinking off, 19 and 22 for the two Ministral runs, 2--18 per Ministral free run with the computation block (Appendix~\ref{app:computed}). No hyperparameter search was performed; the sampling settings are the model vendors' recommended values.
\paragraph{Statistics.} Every contrast is a mean over cue pairs with a 2,000-resample percentile bootstrap that resamples households (clusters) with replacement; balanced accuracy is the mean of the YES-recall and NO-recall. Single runs are reported as such; Table~\ref{tab:comp} averages two seeds where two exist.
\paragraph{Data and licences.} Household structures come from the USDA SNAP Quality Control FY2024 public-use file (U.S. federal public-domain data, de-identified; not redistributed: the file is downloaded from the USDA site and placed where the generator reads it); income limits and deductions from the FNS FY2026 cost-of-living memoranda (public domain). The gold cross-check uses PolicyEngine-US 2.24.5 (AGPL-3.0). The supplementary software (rules-as-code, generator, runners, scorers, tests) and data (6,376 items, the computation-supply and confirmation items, every model output with the generated text and, where the model returns it, the reasoning trace; GPT-5's reasoning is not returned by the API; scores) are released under the licence stated in their README; the items contain no real individuals (placeholder names).

\section{PolicyEngine cross-check scope}\label{app:pe}
500 random households from the generator were run through PolicyEngine-US 2.24.5 with the imputations our packet does not model switched off (automatic SSI/TANF, imputed Medicare premiums, benefit-receipt disability definition, member exclusions). Within scope, 0 mismatches on gross income, the deductions exercised, adjusted and net income, the gross and net tests and the maximum allotment; 29 cases differ by \$1 in the 30\% contribution (rounding convention). Not verified by the cross-check: the ABAWD path (work requirement fixed to met in PolicyEngine), positive medical, dependent-care and child-support deductions (set to 0 in the sample), and asset-limit boundaries; these are covered by hand-written unit tests only.
