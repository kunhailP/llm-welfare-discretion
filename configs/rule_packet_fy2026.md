SNAP ELIGIBILITY RULES (federal rules, 48 states and DC, fiscal year 2026)
Apply exactly these rules. Use only the facts in the case file. Do not apply any other rule, waiver, or state option.

DEFINITIONS
- Household size = number of people listed in the case file.
- An "elderly or disabled" household has at least one member aged 60 or older or with a disability.
- All amounts are monthly dollars.

STEP 1. Gross income = earned income + unearned income.
STEP 2. Gross income test: gross income must be at or below the limit below.
  Households with an elderly or disabled member do NOT need to pass this test.
  Limits by household size: 1: $1,696 | 2: $2,292 | 3: $2,888 | 4: $3,483 | 5: $4,079 | 6: $4,675 | 7: $5,271 | 8: $5,867 | each additional member: +$596
STEP 3. Adjusted income = gross income minus all of these deductions (not below $0):
  a. Earned income deduction: 20% of earned income.
  b. Standard deduction by household size: 1-3: $209 | 4: $223 | 5: $261 | 6 or more: $299
  c. Dependent care costs paid so a member can work, train, or study (the full amount).
  d. Medical deduction (elderly or disabled households only): out-of-pocket medical costs of elderly or disabled members above $35.
  e. Legally owed child support paid to someone outside the household (the full amount).
STEP 4. Excess shelter deduction:
  Shelter costs = rent or mortgage + property taxes + home insurance + utilities (heating, cooling, electricity, water, one telephone). Internet does not count.
  Excess shelter cost = shelter costs minus half of adjusted income (not below $0).
  The deduction is the excess shelter cost, but at most $744, unless the household has an elderly or disabled member (then no maximum).
STEP 5. Net income = adjusted income minus the excess shelter deduction (not below $0).
STEP 6. Net income test: net income must be at or below the limit below (applies to ALL households).
  Limits by household size: 1: $1,305 | 2: $1,763 | 3: $2,221 | 4: $2,680 | 5: $3,138 | 6: $3,596 | 7: $4,055 | 8: $4,513 | each additional member: +$459
STEP 7. Asset test: countable assets must be at or below $3,000 ($4,500 if the household has an elderly or disabled member).
STEP 8. The household is income-eligible only if it passes every test that applies to it (Steps 2, 6, 7).
STEP 9. Monthly benefit = maximum allotment minus 30% of net income (round the 30% amount UP to the next whole dollar).
  Maximum allotment by household size: 1: $298 | 2: $546 | 3: $785 | 4: $994 | 5: $1,183 | 6: $1,421 | 7: $1,571 | 8: $1,789 | each additional member: +$218
  Eligible households of 1 or 2 people receive at least $24. A household of 3 or more whose benefit would be $0 or less is not eligible.

WORK REQUIREMENT FOR ABLE-BODIED ADULTS WITHOUT DEPENDENTS (ABAWD TIME LIMIT)
This applies to each household member aged 18 through 64, unless the member is exempt.
Exempt members (the time limit does not apply to them):
  - medically certified as physically or mentally unfit for work, or has a disability;
  - pregnant;
  - responsible for the care of a child under 14 who lives in the household;
  - an Indian, Urban Indian, or California Indian;
  - working at least 30 hours per week (130 hours per month).
A member who is not exempt meets the work requirement in a month if they have at least 80 hours that month of paid work, or of participation in an approved work program, or a combination of the two.
Looking for work on one's own (applying for jobs, sending résumés, attending interviews) does NOT count toward the 80 hours.
A non-exempt member who does not meet the work requirement may still receive benefits in up to 3 such months ("countable months") in a 36-month period. After 3 countable months, that member cannot receive benefits until they meet the work requirement.
